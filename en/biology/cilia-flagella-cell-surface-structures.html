Cilia, flagella and the structures of the cell surface

A classic General Biology question: when you see multiple structures around a cell, are they cilia or flagella? Settle it in 2 minutes, with the comparison table and the exam traps.

Direct answer: The multiple, short structures surrounding the cell are cilia. Flagella are long and few (one or a few per cell). Both share the same internal microtubule structure (the 9+2 axoneme), but they differ in length, number and type of movement.

What surrounds or projects from the cell surface?

A cell's surface is not just a smooth bilayer. Depending on the cell type, it can display several external structures with different functions:

  • Cilia — motile projections that are short and numerous. They beat in a coordinated way.
  • Flagella — motile projections that are long and few (typically 1). They move by undulating.
  • Microvilli — non-motile projections that increase the absorptive surface.
  • Glycocalyx — outer carbohydrate coat for cell recognition and protection.
  • Cell wall — rigid supporting cover in plants, fungi and bacteria.

Internal structure: the 9+2 axoneme

Both eukaryotic cilia and flagella share the same microtubule skeleton, called the axoneme:

  • 9 outer doublets of microtubules plus 2 central microtubules → the 9+2 pattern.
  • They are anchored in the basal body, which has a 9+0 structure (9 triplets, just like a centriole).
  • Movement is generated by the motor protein dynein, which slides the microtubules using ATP.
Common exam trap: the bacterial flagellum (prokaryotic) does not have a 9+2 structure. It is made of the protein flagellin and rotates like a propeller, powered by the proton-motive force. Don't confuse it with the eukaryotic flagellum (9+2, which undulates). This is a classic point-losing mistake.

Other cell surface structures

Microvilli

Short non-motile projections with a core of actin filaments (not microtubules). They increase the surface area for absorption; they form the "brush border" of the intestinal epithelium. Don't confuse them with cilia: microvilli do not move.

Glycocalyx

A layer of carbohydrates (glycoproteins and glycolipids) on the membrane. Functions: cell recognition, adhesion and protection.

Cell wall

Rigid outer cover: cellulose in plants, chitin in fungi and peptidoglycan in bacteria. It provides support and protects against osmotic pressure.

Frequently asked questions

Which multiple structures surround the cell?

The multiple, short surface structures are cilia. If they are long and few, they are flagella. The surface may also have a glycocalyx, microvilli or a cell wall.

What is the difference between cilia and flagella?

Cilia are short and numerous and beat like oars; flagella are long and few and move by undulating. Both have a 9+2 axoneme.

Are cilia and microvilli the same thing?

No. Cilia move (microtubules, 9+2); microvilli do not move (actin) and are used for absorption.
