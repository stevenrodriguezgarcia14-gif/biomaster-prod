Difference between mitosis and meiosis

The star comparison in General Biology. Here is the mitosis–meiosis difference in a table, the phases of each and the trap that costs points.

Direct answer: Mitosis is 1 division and produces 2 diploid (2n) daughter cells identical to the parent; it is for growth and repair. Meiosis is 2 divisions and produces 4 haploid (n) daughter cells that are genetically different; it forms gametes and creates variability.

Phases of mitosis (PMAT)

Prophase (chromosomes condense) → Metaphase (align at the equator) → Anaphase (sister chromatids separate) → Telophase (2 nuclei form), followed by cytokinesis.

Phases of meiosis

Meiosis I (reductional): in prophase I, crossing-over occurs between homologous chromosomes; in anaphase I the homologues separate. Reduces 2n to n.

Meiosis II (equational): similar to a mitosis; sister chromatids separate. Result: 4 haploid cells.

Exam trap: the reduction in chromosome number happens in meiosis I (homologues separate), NOT in meiosis II. In meiosis II sister chromatids separate, just like in mitosis.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference between mitosis and meiosis?

Mitosis produces 2 identical diploid cells; meiosis produces 4 different haploid cells with genetic variability.

How many daughter cells does each produce?

Mitosis produces 2 daughter cells; meiosis, 4.

In which phase is the chromosome number reduced?

In meiosis I (anaphase I), when the homologous chromosomes separate.
