Photosynthesis vs cellular respiration

Two processes that look opposite and so get confused. Here you see their equations, where they occur and why they actually complement each other.

Direct answer: Photosynthesis occurs in the chloroplasts and builds glucose from CO₂, water and light, releasing O₂ (anabolic process, stores energy). Cellular respiration occurs in the mitochondria and breaks down glucose with O₂ to release energy (ATP), producing CO₂ and water (catabolic process). They are inverse, complementary processes.

Equations

Photosynthesis: 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂

Cellular respiration: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + ATP

Notice that one is essentially the reverse reaction of the other.

Exam trap: plants do both. Photosynthesis does not "replace" respiration: the plant makes glucose during the day and respires it (in its mitochondria) day and night to get ATP.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between photosynthesis and cellular respiration?

Photosynthesis builds glucose and stores energy in the chloroplast; cellular respiration breaks down glucose and releases energy (ATP) in the mitochondrion.

Are they opposite processes?

They are inverse and complementary: their equations are nearly opposite and together they form a cycle of energy and matter.

Do plants also respire?

Yes. Plants carry out both photosynthesis and cellular respiration; they respire day and night.
