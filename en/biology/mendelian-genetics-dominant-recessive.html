Mendelian genetics: dominant, recessive and the Punnett square

The basics of Mendelian inheritance. Alleles, genotype, phenotype and how to predict crosses with the Punnett square.

Direct answer: A dominant allele (written uppercase, e.g. A) is expressed whenever it is present; a recessive allele (lowercase, a) is only expressed when it is homozygous (aa). The genotype is the combination of alleles and the phenotype is the visible trait.

Key concepts

  • Homozygous: two identical alleles (AA or aa).
  • Heterozygous: two different alleles (Aa) — shows the dominant phenotype.
  • Genotype: the alleles it has (AA, Aa, aa).
  • Phenotype: the observable trait (e.g. purple or white flower).

Punnett square (Aa × Aa)

Aa
AAAAa
aAaaa

Result: 3:1 in phenotype (3 dominant : 1 recessive) and 1:2:1 in genotype (1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa).

Exam trap: the heterozygote Aa shows the dominant phenotype (not a blend, except in incomplete dominance). The recessive trait only appears in aa.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between genotype and phenotype?

The genotype is the combination of alleles (AA, Aa, aa); the phenotype is the observable trait.

What ratio does an Aa × Aa cross give?

3:1 in phenotype and 1:2:1 in genotype.

When is a recessive allele expressed?

Only when homozygous (aa), when there is no dominant allele to mask it.
