Stages of cellular respiration: glycolysis, Krebs and electron transport

How the cell gets ~36 ATP from one glucose, step by step. The three stages, where they occur and how much energy each yields.

Direct answer: Cellular respiration has 3 stages: glycolysis (in the cytoplasm; glucose → 2 pyruvate, +2 ATP), the Krebs cycle (in the mitochondrial matrix; releases CO₂ and NADH/FADH₂) and the electron transport chain (on the mitochondrial cristae; uses O₂ and produces most of the ATP, ~34).

Table of the 3 stages

StageWhereProduces
GlycolysisCytoplasm2 pyruvate, 2 ATP, 2 NADH
Krebs cycleMitochondrial matrixCO₂, NADH, FADH₂, 2 ATP
Electron transport chainCristae (inner membrane)~34 ATP, H₂O

Approximate total yield: ~36–38 ATP per glucose. The final electron acceptor is oxygen.

Exam trap: most of the ATP is produced in the electron transport chain (oxidative phosphorylation), not in glycolysis. Without oxygen, the cell resorts to fermentation and gets far less ATP.

Frequently asked questions

What are the stages of cellular respiration?

Glycolysis, the Krebs cycle and the electron transport chain.

Where does each stage occur?

Glycolysis in the cytoplasm; Krebs in the mitochondrial matrix; the electron transport chain on the mitochondrial cristae.

How much ATP is produced in total?

Approximately 36–38 ATP per glucose molecule.
