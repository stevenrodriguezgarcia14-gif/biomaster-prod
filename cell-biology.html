Question 1 — Organelles

Which organelle is responsible for synthesizing proteins that are destined to be secreted from the cell or inserted into the plasma membrane?

A Free ribosomes in the cytoplasm
B Rough endoplasmic reticulum (rough ER) ✓
C Smooth endoplasmic reticulum (smooth ER)
D Lysosome
The rough ER is studded with ribosomes that synthesize proteins destined for secretion, membrane insertion, or the endomembrane pathway. After synthesis, these proteins enter the ER lumen, are folded, modified, and then transported to the Golgi apparatus for further processing. Free ribosomes synthesize proteins that stay in the cytoplasm.
Question 2 — Plasma Membrane

The fluid mosaic model of the plasma membrane describes it as composed primarily of:

A A rigid protein layer with embedded lipids
B A fluid phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins ✓
C A single phospholipid monolayer surrounded by proteins
D Cellulose and proteins in equal proportions
In the fluid mosaic model (Singer & Nicolson, 1972), the membrane is a dynamic bilayer of phospholipids with hydrophilic heads facing outward (aqueous environments) and hydrophobic tails pointing inward. Proteins float within or on this bilayer. Cholesterol regulates fluidity. The term "mosaic" refers to the protein diversity; "fluid" means the components can move laterally.
Question 3 — Eukaryotes vs. Prokaryotes

Which of the following is a key feature that distinguishes eukaryotic cells from prokaryotic cells?

A Prokaryotes have ribosomes; eukaryotes do not
B Prokaryotes have a plasma membrane; eukaryotes do not
C Eukaryotes have a membrane-bound nucleus; prokaryotes do not ✓
D Eukaryotes are always unicellular; prokaryotes are always multicellular
The defining feature of eukaryotes is the membrane-bound nucleus housing the cell's DNA. Prokaryotes (bacteria and archaea) have their DNA in a nucleoid region without a membrane. Both cell types have ribosomes and plasma membranes. Eukaryotes also have membrane-bound organelles (mitochondria, ER, Golgi, etc.) absent in prokaryotes.
Question 4 — Mitochondria

The inner membrane of the mitochondria (cristae) is the site of which critical cellular process?

A Glycolysis
B The Krebs cycle (TCA cycle)
C The electron transport chain (ETC) ✓
D DNA replication
The inner mitochondrial membrane (folded into cristae) hosts the electron transport chain protein complexes (I–IV) and ATP synthase. Electrons from NADH/FADH₂ move through these complexes, pumping H⁺ across the membrane to drive ATP synthase. The Krebs cycle occurs in the mitochondrial matrix. Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm.
Question 5 — Golgi Apparatus

What is the primary function of the Golgi apparatus in the cell?

A DNA storage and replication
B Processing, modifying, and packaging proteins for secretion or transport ✓
C Lipid synthesis
D Protein synthesis using mRNA
The Golgi apparatus acts as the cell's "post office." Proteins from the rough ER arrive at the cis face, pass through stacked cisternae where they are modified (glycosylation, phosphorylation), then are sorted and packaged into vesicles at the trans face for delivery to lysosomes, the plasma membrane, or secretion outside the cell.
Question 6 — Cell Cycle

DNA replication occurs during which phase of the cell cycle?

A G1 phase (first gap phase)
B S phase (synthesis phase) ✓
C G2 phase (second gap phase)
D M phase (mitosis)
The cell cycle has: G1 (cell growth, protein synthesis) → S phase (DNA synthesis — genome duplicated) → G2 (preparation for division, organelle duplication) → M phase (mitosis + cytokinesis). After S phase, the cell has 4N DNA content (tetraploid) before dividing back to 2N. The G1/S checkpoint ensures DNA integrity before replication begins.
Question 7 — Lysosomes

Lysosomes contain hydrolytic enzymes that function best at a low pH (~5). What does this imply about their interior environment?

A The lysosome interior is alkaline (basic)
B The lysosome interior is neutral (pH 7)
C The lysosome interior is acidic ✓
D The lysosome maintains the same pH as the cytoplasm
Lysosomes maintain an acidic interior (~pH 4.5–5) using proton pumps that move H⁺ into the lumen. This acidic environment activates the 50+ hydrolytic enzymes inside (proteases, lipases, nucleases, glycosidases) that digest cellular waste, old organelles (autophagy), and pathogens (phagocytosis). The low pH also protects the cell — if a lysosome leaks, the neutral cytoplasm inactivates the enzymes.
Question 8 — Membrane Transport

Which transport mechanism moves large molecules into the cell by forming a vesicle from the plasma membrane — without requiring membrane proteins?

A Simple diffusion
B Facilitated diffusion
C Active transport
D Endocytosis ✓
Endocytosis (phagocytosis, pinocytosis, and receptor-mediated endocytosis) engulfs large particles or liquids by folding the plasma membrane inward to form an intracellular vesicle. This does not use channel or carrier proteins (unlike facilitated diffusion or active transport). It requires ATP. The reverse process — exocytosis — exports material by fusing vesicles with the plasma membrane.
