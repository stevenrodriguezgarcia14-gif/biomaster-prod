Question 1 — Cell Biology

Which organelle is known as the "powerhouse of the cell" because it produces most of the cell's ATP supply?

A Ribosome
B Mitochondria ✓
C Nucleus
D Lysosome
The mitochondria produces ATP via the electron transport chain and Krebs cycle. A single glucose molecule yields ~36–38 ATP through complete aerobic respiration.
Question 2 — Genetics

In a monohybrid cross between two heterozygous parents (Aa × Aa), what is the expected phenotype ratio in the offspring?

A 1:1
B 1:2:1
C 3:1 ✓
D 1:1:1:1
The Punnett square gives AA (25%), Aa (50%), aa (25%). Since A is dominant, ¾ show the dominant phenotype and ¼ show the recessive phenotype → 3:1 ratio.
Question 3 — Photosynthesis

Where does the Calvin cycle (light-independent reactions) take place within the chloroplast?

A Thylakoid membrane
B Stroma ✓
C Outer chloroplast membrane
D Intermembrane space
The light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes. The Calvin cycle uses the ATP and NADPH produced there to fix CO₂ into glucose in the stroma (the fluid-filled space around the thylakoids).
Question 4 — Molecular Biology

Which enzyme is responsible for unwinding the DNA double helix during replication?

A DNA polymerase
B Ligase
C Helicase ✓
D Primase
Helicase breaks the hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs to unwind and separate the double helix. DNA polymerase then synthesizes new strands using each separated strand as a template. Primase synthesizes the RNA primer that starts replication.
Question 5 — Cell Division

What is the main difference between mitosis and meiosis in terms of the cells produced?

A Mitosis produces 4 cells; meiosis produces 2 cells
B Mitosis produces 2 diploid cells; meiosis produces 4 haploid cells ✓
C Mitosis produces haploid cells; meiosis produces diploid cells
D Both produce 4 diploid cells
Mitosis is for growth/repair — produces 2 genetically identical diploid (2n) daughter cells. Meiosis is for sexual reproduction — produces 4 genetically diverse haploid (n) gametes through 2 rounds of division and crossing over.
Question 6 — Metabolism

Which molecule serves as the primary energy currency of the cell, used to power most cellular processes?

A ATP (adenosine triphosphate) ✓
B ADP (adenosine diphosphate)
C NADH
D Glucose
ATP is the universal energy currency. Energy is released when the terminal phosphate bond is hydrolyzed (ATP → ADP + Pi). Glucose is a fuel source, but ATP is what directly powers cellular work. NADH is an electron carrier used to produce more ATP.
Question 7 — Enzymes

What happens to an enzyme after it catalyzes a chemical reaction?

A It is permanently destroyed
B It becomes part of the product
C It is released unchanged and can catalyze another reaction ✓
D It fuses with the substrate permanently
Enzymes are biological catalysts — they speed up reactions without being consumed. The enzyme binds its substrate at the active site, lowers the activation energy, releases the product, and is then free to bind another substrate molecule. A single enzyme molecule can catalyze thousands of reactions per second.
Question 8 — Evolution

According to Darwin's theory of natural selection, which individuals are most likely to survive and reproduce?

A The largest individuals in the population
B The oldest individuals with the most experience
C Individuals with traits best suited to their environment ✓
D Individuals that reproduce most rapidly regardless of traits
Natural selection favors individuals with heritable traits that improve their fitness (survival + reproductive success) in a specific environment. These traits become more common in the population over generations. Size or age alone is not the determining factor — environmental fit is.
