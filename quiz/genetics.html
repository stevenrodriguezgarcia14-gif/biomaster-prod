Question 1 — Law of Segregation

Mendel's Law of Segregation states that allele pairs separate during gamete formation. A plant with genotype Aa will produce gametes in what ratio?

A All gametes carry A
B 50% carry A, 50% carry a ✓
C 75% carry A, 25% carry a
D All gametes carry a
During meiosis, the Aa parent's homologous chromosomes separate. Each gamete receives exactly one allele — half receive A and half receive a. This is the foundation of Mendelian genetics.
Question 2 — Monohybrid Cross

Two heterozygous tall plants (Tt × Tt) are crossed, where tall (T) is dominant over short (t). What fraction of offspring will be short?

A 1/2 (50%)
B 1/4 (25%) ✓
C 3/4 (75%)
D 0% — all offspring will be tall
Punnett square: TT (1), Tt (2), tt (1). Only tt is short (recessive phenotype). That's ¼ of offspring. The 3:1 dominant:recessive phenotype ratio is Mendel's classic result and a fundamental exam concept.
Question 3 — Genotype vs. Phenotype

Which of the following correctly defines "genotype"?

A The physical appearance or observable traits of an organism
B The specific allele combination an organism carries for a gene ✓
C The dominant allele that masks the recessive one
D The environment-influenced expression of a gene
Genotype = the actual alleles (e.g., TT, Tt, tt). Phenotype = the observable result (tall or short). Two organisms with different genotypes (TT and Tt) can have the same phenotype (both tall) because T is dominant. Environment can modify phenotype but not genotype.
Question 4 — Codominance

In codominance, when two different alleles are present in a heterozygous individual, the resulting phenotype is:

A The dominant allele's trait only
B A blend of both traits (intermediate)
C Both alleles are fully and simultaneously expressed ✓
D Neither allele is expressed
Codominance differs from incomplete dominance (blending). In codominance, both phenotypes appear simultaneously — like AB blood type, where both A and B antigens are expressed equally on red blood cells. Think of a roan horse (both red and white hairs present, not pink).
Question 5 — Test Cross

A biologist wants to determine if a dominant-phenotype plant is homozygous (TT) or heterozygous (Tt). Which cross should they perform?

A Cross it with another dominant-phenotype plant (TT)
B Cross it with a homozygous recessive plant (tt) ✓
C Self-pollinate it
D Cross it with a heterozygous plant (Tt)
A test cross uses a homozygous recessive (tt) parent. If the unknown is TT: all offspring are Tt (100% tall). If the unknown is Tt: half offspring are Tt (tall) and half are tt (short) → 50:50 ratio reveals heterozygosity. This is the classic way to determine unknown genotype.
Question 6 — Law of Independent Assortment

Mendel's Law of Independent Assortment applies to genes located on:

A Different (non-homologous) chromosomes ✓
B The same chromosome, close together
C The X chromosome only
D The same chromosome locus
Independent assortment applies when genes are on different chromosomes (or far apart on the same chromosome). During meiosis I, homologous chromosome pairs align independently, so the alleles of one gene pair don't influence the distribution of another gene pair. Genes on the same chromosome are said to be "linked" and don't assort independently.
Question 7 — Sex-Linked Inheritance

Color blindness is an X-linked recessive trait. A color-blind father (X^b Y) and a carrier mother (X^B X^b) have children. What is the probability that a son will be color blind?

A 0%
B 25%
C 50% ✓
D 100%
Sons get their X chromosome only from the mother. The carrier mother (X^B X^b) passes X^B to 50% of sons and X^b to the other 50%. Sons who receive X^b are color blind (X^b Y). The father's X^b only goes to daughters. So 50% of sons are affected.
Question 8 — Mutation

A mutation that changes a single nucleotide base in a codon, resulting in a different amino acid being incorporated into the protein, is called a:

A Silent (synonymous) mutation
B Nonsense mutation
C Missense mutation ✓
D Frameshift mutation
A missense mutation changes one base → different codon → different amino acid → potentially altered protein function. A silent mutation changes the base but codes for the same amino acid (due to codon redundancy). A nonsense mutation creates a premature stop codon. A frameshift shifts the entire reading frame.
